The VA loan benefit has no expiration date and no limit on the number of times it can be used — a service member can use it at every duty station over an entire career. Entitlement is restored when the home bought with the VA loan is sold and the loan paid in full, and with full entitlement VA sets no loan limit. With remaining entitlement, a second VA loan is possible while the first is still open — a common scenario when PCSing before selling a previous home.

The single biggest misconception about the VA loan benefit is that it's a one-time deal. It isn't. A service member who buys at [Fort Bragg](/bah/fort-bragg), PCSs to [Fort Hood](/bah/fort-hood), buys again, PCSs to [JBLM](/bah/joint-base-lewis-mcchord), buys again — can use the VA loan all three times. The entitlement system allows repeated use, which is why many service members use VA financing at more than one duty station.

*This post is educational. MilPayTools is not a lender and does not provide mortgage advice. Entitlement, restoration rules, and loan requirements vary by situation — confirm with a VA-approved lender or VA.gov for your specific circumstances.*

## What entitlement actually is

Entitlement is the part of the loan VA guarantees to your lender.

**Basic entitlement** covers a loan of <VaEntitlementFigure show="basicMax" /> or less. **For loans above that,** VA guarantees up to 25% of the loan amount, and bonus entitlement covers the rest of that guaranty.

**Full entitlement:** if you've never used a VA loan, or your entitlement has been restored, you have full entitlement, and VA says you don't have a loan limit. That isn't unlimited buying power: your lender still has to approve the loan (your credit, income, debts and assets), and the appraisal has to support the price.

**Remaining entitlement:** if a prior VA loan still uses part of your entitlement, what's left is 25% of the county loan limit, minus the entitlement already used and not restored. VA's loan limits are FHFA's conforming loan limits, set by county; VA says to use the One-Unit limit even if the property has more than one unit.

Your Certificate of Eligibility (COE) shows the entitlement already used, in the table "Prior Loans charged to entitlement", and confirms your eligibility to a lender.

## How entitlement is restored

Previously used entitlement is restored in one of three ways.

**Sell and pay off:** the home you bought with the prior VA loan has been sold and the loan paid in full. This is the most common path.

**One-time restoration — when you keep the property:** once only, if you've repaid the prior VA loan in full, even if you haven't sold the home. For example, you paid off a VA loan early and kept the home as a rental. Because it's available once, confirm with VA or your lender before you rely on it.

**Assumption with substitution of entitlement:** a qualified Veteran buyer agrees to assume your VA loan and substitute their entitlement for yours. If the buyer doesn't substitute entitlement (for example, a buyer who isn't VA-eligible), the entitlement stays with that loan. For the full mechanics — buyer qualification, the <VaFundingFee type="assumption" show="rate" /> assumption funding fee, and what sellers should confirm before accepting an assumption offer — see [VA Loan Assumptions Explained](/blog/va-loan-assumptions-explained).

## Using two VA loans at the same time

This scenario comes up frequently at PCS: you need to buy at your new station, but you haven't sold your current home yet. Can you carry two VA loans simultaneously?

**The answer is yes — if you have enough remaining entitlement.**

Here's how it works. The entitlement your first VA loan uses stays with that loan until it's restored. VA's rule for what's left:

1. Find the entitlement already used on your COE ("Prior Loans charged to entitlement").
2. Look up the One-Unit loan limit for the county where you're buying (FHFA).
3. Multiply it by 0.25.
4. Subtract the entitlement already used. The result is your remaining entitlement.

Most lenders lend up to 4 times your remaining entitlement with no down payment. For a larger loan, your lender may require a down payment: most lenders want your entitlement and a down payment together to cover at least 25% of the loan.

**Example** (a county at the <VaEntitlementFigure show="year" /> limit for most U.S. counties):

|---|---|
| Existing VA loan original amount | $280,000 |

## The subsequent use funding fee

Using the VA benefit again doesn't erase the advantage — but it does raise the funding fee.

| Down Payment | First Use | Subsequent Use |
|---|---|---|
| Less than 5% | <VaFundingFee use="first" down="0" show="rate" /> | <VaFundingFee use="subsequent" down="0" show="rate" /> |
| 5% to 9.99% | <VaFundingFee use="first" down="5" show="rate" /> | <VaFundingFee use="subsequent" down="5" show="rate" /> |
| 10% or more | <VaFundingFee use="first" down="10" show="rate" /> | <VaFundingFee use="subsequent" down="10" show="rate" /> |

At 5% or more down, the fee is identical regardless of first or subsequent use. The higher subsequent-use rate only applies when putting less than 5% down.

- 10% down: <VaFundingFee use="subsequent" down="10" show="rate" /> = **<VaFundingFee price="350000" use="subsequent" down="10" /> fee** on the <VaFundingFee price="350000" use="subsequent" down="10" show="loan" /> loan (down payment: <VaFundingFee price="350000" use="subsequent" down="10" show="down" />)

Funding fee exemptions apply on every use — first, subsequent, and refinance. VA's rule: you don't pay the funding fee if you receive VA compensation for a service-connected disability; you're eligible for that compensation but receive retirement or active-duty pay instead; you receive Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC) as the surviving spouse of a Veteran; you're a service member with a proposed or memorandum rating, dated before closing, that says you're eligible for compensation because of a pre-discharge claim; or you're on active duty and, on or before closing, provide evidence that you received a Purple Heart. Confirm your exemption status with your lender.

See [VA Loan Funding Fee Explained](/blog/va-loan-funding-fee-explained) for additional exemption details.

## Comparing VA and conventional on a second purchase

The <VaFundingFee use="subsequent" down="0" show="rate" /> subsequent-use funding fee is real money. At <VaFundingFee price="350000" use="subsequent" down="0" /> on a $350,000 loan, it's more than the first-use fee by <VaFundingFee price="350000" use="subsequent" down="0" vsUse="first" />. Financed over 30 years at 6.5%, the fee adds about <VaFundingFee price="350000" use="subsequent" down="0" show="monthly" interest="6.5" years="30" /> a month to the payment.

A VA loan has no monthly mortgage insurance; a conventional loan with less than 20% down usually adds private mortgage insurance (PMI). Which costs less depends on your rate, down payment, how long you keep the home, and whether you're exempt from the funding fee. Compare actual Loan Estimates from VA and conventional lenders.

## An IRRRL reuses the entitlement already tied to your existing VA loan

If you're already in a VA loan and refinancing with an IRRRL, you're not using the VA benefit "again" in the entitlement sense — you're replacing the existing VA loan with a new one. The same entitlement that was tied to the original loan carries over to the IRRRL.

The IRRRL also carries only a <VaFundingFee type="irrrl" show="rate" /> funding fee — far lower than any purchase fee, first or subsequent. See the [VA Refinance Calculator](/calculators/va-refinance) to estimate whether the rate reduction pays off before your next move.

## Before you use your VA loan again

- Pull your current Certificate of Eligibility (COE)
- Confirm whether your entitlement is full or partial
- Check whether a prior VA loan is still tying up entitlement
- Confirm whether you are funding-fee exempt
- Estimate the funding fee for first vs subsequent use
- Work out your remaining entitlement in the county where you're buying ([VA Loan Entitlement Calculator](/calculators/va-entitlement)), then ask your lender whether it supports $0 down on your target price
- If carrying two properties, budget for the risk of vacancy, PCS timing, and two mortgage payments simultaneously
- Compare VA against conventional using actual Loan Estimates, not generic assumptions

## The bottom line

The benefit does not expire, but entitlement can be tied up in an existing VA loan until it is restored. Every eligible service member can — in theory — use it at every duty station over a full career. The mechanics that matter: sell and pay off to restore full entitlement, know your remaining entitlement in the county where you're buying before carrying two loans at once, and compare the <VaFundingFee use="subsequent" down="0" show="rate" /> subsequent-use fee with a conventional loan's costs on your own numbers.
