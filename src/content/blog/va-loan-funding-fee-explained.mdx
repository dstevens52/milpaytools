## What the funding fee is

The VA funding fee is a one-time charge paid to the Department of Veterans Affairs. You pay it at closing or roll it into the loan.

The fee applies to all VA loan types (purchase, refinance, cash-out) unless the borrower is exempt. The funding fee is calculated as a percentage of the VA base loan amount before the funding fee is added. On a purchase, that usually means the purchase price minus any down payment — not the home value and not the final financed loan amount after the funding fee is rolled in.

## Who is exempt from the VA funding fee

VA's rule: you won't have to pay a VA funding fee if any of these is true for you:

- **You're receiving VA compensation for a service-connected disability** — the most common exemption
- **You're eligible to receive VA compensation for a service-connected disability, but you're receiving retirement or active-duty pay instead**
- **You're receiving Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC)** as the surviving spouse of a Veteran
- **You're a service member who has received a proposed or memorandum rating before the loan closing date** that says you're eligible to get compensation because of a pre-discharge claim
- **You're an active-duty member of the Armed Forces, and on or before your loan closing date you provide evidence that you received a Purple Heart**

**What the exemption is worth.** On a $350,000 first-use purchase loan with no down payment, the exemption removes a <VaFundingFee price="350000" use="first" down="0" /> fee. On a subsequent-use loan at the same amount, it removes <VaFundingFee price="350000" use="subsequent" down="0" />.

The exemption follows compensation, not a rating number: a 0% rating pays no compensation, so on its own it doesn't exempt anyone. If your disability claim is still pending at closing, the fee is charged at closing — VA's refund rule (below) may apply later.

## What happens if your disability claim is pending at closing

VA's refund rule: you may be eligible for a refund of the VA funding fee if you're later awarded VA compensation for a service-connected disability. The effective date of your VA compensation must be retroactive to before the date of your loan closing.

This applies when:
1. You paid the funding fee at closing (or it was rolled into the loan)
2. Your disability compensation was later approved
3. The effective date of the award predates your loan closing

One timing rule cuts the other way: if you get a proposed or memorandum rating after your loan closing date, you still pay the funding fee, and that rating doesn't qualify you for a refund. A proposed or memorandum rating dated before closing is on VA's exemption list (above). See [File Your VA Disability Claim Before You Separate](/blog/file-va-disability-before-separation) for how the timing of disability claims works.

## Rolling the fee into the loan vs. paying at closing

You can roll the funding fee into the loan instead of paying it at closing. Rolling it in removes one more line item from the cash needed to close.

**The tradeoff:** Rolling the fee in increases your loan balance and your monthly payment for the life of the loan.

## What can be rolled into a VA loan

On a VA purchase loan, the funding fee can be rolled into the loan. Whether other closing costs — appraisal, title, escrow, prepaids, recording fees, discount points, inspections, and lender charges — can be financed or covered by seller or lender credits depends on the loan type and on VA's and your lender's rules. Ask your lender which costs on your Loan Estimate are financed and which are due at closing.

## The IRRRL funding fee

If you already have a VA loan and rates have dropped, the IRRRL (Interest Rate Reduction Refinance Loan) carries a <VaFundingFee type="irrrl" show="rate" /> funding fee — the same rate as a loan assumption, and lower than any purchase or cash-out rate. On a $300,000 remaining balance, that's <VaFundingFee type="irrrl" loan="300000" />.

VA generally doesn't require an appraisal or credit underwriting for an IRRRL. Lenders can still require them under their own rules. See the [VA Refinance Calculator](/calculators/va-refinance) to estimate monthly savings and the months to recover closing costs.

## Where to verify the funding fee on your loan documents

Look for the VA funding fee on your Loan Estimate (when shopping) and Closing Disclosure (before signing). Confirm three things: the fee rate used, whether the fee is financed or paid in cash, and whether your exemption status is correctly reflected. If anything looks wrong, raise it with your lender before closing.
