If you've got PCS orders, you've probably heard someone in your unit swear they made a few thousand dollars on a "DITY move" — and someone else call it the most stressful month of their life. Both are usually telling the truth. A PPM can be genuinely worth it, but the answer depends on numbers specific to your move, and on whether the work and the tax bill are worth the payoff to you. Here's how to find your number.

## What a PPM actually is

A PPM (Personally Procured Move), formerly known as a DITY (Do-It-Yourself) move, means you move some or all of your household goods yourself instead of having the government contract a moving company. In exchange, you get an incentive of 100% of the Government Constructed Cost (GCC): what it would have cost the government to move the same weight the same distance. The GCC is computed for each move, so there's no single rate per pound. The gap between the incentive and what you actually spend — truck rental, fuel, packing supplies — is what you keep.

One detail that catches people off guard: the incentive is taxable income. That changes the math, so it's covered below.

## The part most people get wrong

Here's the misconception that trips up a lot of first-time movers: they think they're choosing between their PCS entitlements *or* PPM money. You're not.

Your core PCS entitlements — DLA, mileage (MALT), per diem, and TLE — are paid to you the same way regardless of which move type you pick. The PPM incentive comes on top of all of that. So the real comparison isn't "government-move money vs. PPM money." It's "the entitlements you get either way, plus what's left of the incentive after your moving costs if you PPM."

## An example: E-6, Fort Campbell to JBLM

Take an E-6 with a spouse and a child under 12, moving from [Fort Campbell](/bah/fort-campbell), KY to [Joint Base Lewis-McChord](/bah/joint-base-lewis-mcchord), WA — about 2,200 miles, one POV, and 20 days of combined TLE. The [PCS calculator's worked example](/calculators/pcs) computes this move from the current rates: DLA, mileage, per diem for each travel day, and TLE at its daily ceiling, all paid whichever move type the family picks.

The PPM piece sits on top. For a full PPM, the incentive is what it would have cost the government to move the family's goods, up to the E-6 weight allowance, the same distance. Subtract the truck rental, fuel and supplies, and what's left is what the family keeps before tax. The incentive figure comes from the move system (MilMove or DPS) or the transportation office, so get that estimate before you decide.

## When a PPM is worth it — and when it isn't

The math swings on a few things.

It tends to pay off more when:

- You're moving well under your weight allowance. The reimbursement is based on the government's cost to move your authorized weight, but your actual costs scale with how much you really haul. Lighter load, similar reimbursement, lower costs — bigger margin.
- You have the labor. Family help, time, and the physical ability to load and drive change the equation. If you'd have to hire labor at both ends, that eats into the proceeds.
- It's a CONUS move with a reasonable driving distance and affordable truck rental.

It's less attractive when:

- Your household is large and close to (or over) your weight allowance — more to move, higher costs, and any overage comes out of your pocket.
- The move is OCONUS, where different rules apply (TLA instead of TLE, shipping logistics, and more).
- You're on a tight timeline or can't do the physical work, and would have to pay to replace your own labor.

## Don't forget the tax bill

The incentive is taxable income; documented moving costs (truck rental, fuel, packing materials) reduce the taxable part. The practical takeaway: keep every receipt — truck rental, fuel, packing materials, and weight tickets. Those receipts are worth real money.

## How to find your number

That example is one rank, one route, one family. Yours will be different. Two numbers settle it: the entitlements you get either way, and the PPM incentive for your weight and distance. The [PCS calculator](/calculators/pcs) gives you the first from your rank, dependents and distance, along with your weight allowance; your move system or transportation office gives you the second.

A few things to confirm as you plan:

- Your reimbursable mileage is the official DTOD distance, not what your GPS shows.
- A partial PPM is an option — the government ships most of your goods and you self-move a portion for a PPM incentive on that weight, a lower-effort way to capture some of the upside.
- Final entitlements and the PPM incentive are approved by your installation's Transportation Office (TMO) and Finance Office. Confirm your specifics with them before making decisions.

A PPM isn't automatically the right call, and it isn't automatically a windfall — but for a lot of CONUS moves, especially under-weight ones, the difference can be real money for a few weekends of work. Get your numbers first, decide with the actual figures in front of you, and keep your receipts.
