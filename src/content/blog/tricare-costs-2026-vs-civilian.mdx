## Group A or Group B: which costs apply to you?

TRICARE sets two cost levels, and which one applies depends on when the sponsor first joined:

- **Group A:** the sponsor's initial enlistment or appointment began before Jan. 1, 2018.
- **Group B:** the sponsor's initial enlistment or appointment began on or after Jan. 1, 2018.

The premium-based plans (TRICARE Reserve Select, TRICARE Retired Reserve and TRICARE Young Adult) follow TRICARE Select Group B deductibles, copays and catastrophic caps; only their premiums are set separately.

## What does active-duty TRICARE Prime cover?

Active duty members are enrolled in TRICARE Prime and have no out-of-pocket costs: no premium, no enrollment fee, no deductible and no copays for covered care. (Costs for family members are in the active duty families section above.)

**What active-duty members don't pay for:**
- ER visits at MTFs
- Hospitalization
- Mental health services
- Preventive care
- Maternity care
- Pediatric care for dependent children

**Coverage area:** TRICARE follows you anywhere. OCONUS assignments, deployments, TDY — your coverage is continuous. There's no network to "go out of" when you're stationed in Germany or Korea.

## How to count healthcare in compensation comparisons

When comparing a military compensation package to a civilian job offer, three steps keep the healthcare line honest:

**Step 1:** Find the actual cost of family health insurance at the civilian employer: the employee-paid premium, the deductible and the out-of-pocket maximum. Add them up to a realistic annual cost.

**Step 2:** Subtract what you pay for military healthcare now (nothing for active duty members, TRS premiums for Guard and Reserve, enrollment fees for retirees).

**Step 3:** The difference is the healthcare gap the civilian salary has to cover.

## What about TRICARE For Life?

At 65, military retirees with Medicare Part A and Part B qualify for TRICARE For Life, which works as a supplement to Medicare and covers most of what Medicare doesn't. The main cost is the Medicare Part B premium, which most people over 65 pay anyway; there's no separate TRICARE For Life enrollment fee. TRICARE posts 2027 TRICARE For Life costs early in 2027.
